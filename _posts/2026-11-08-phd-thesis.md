In May 2025 I defended my PhD thesis in Physics at the University of Bologna,
[**Data analytics and predictive models for sustainable multimodal mobility in future smart cities**](https://amsdottorato.unibo.it/id/eprint/12316/).
It was supervised by Armando Bazzani, with Mirko Degli Esposti as co-supervisor. This is a short
overview for anyone who doesn't want to read the full manuscript.

## The question

Cities collect more and more data about how people move: Wi-Fi access points detect phones,
mobile networks log connections, and roads are fitted with sensors that count vehicles. How can we
turn these heterogeneous, noisy and incomplete sources into **models that actually predict
mobility**, and that can support planning and real-time decisions?

## Two scales, two case studies

The thesis follows the same approach at two very different scales.

**Pedestrians in heritage cities.** Historic centres such as **Ferrara**, **Šibenik** and
**Dubrovnik** attract large flows of visitors in small, fragile spaces. Wi-Fi and mobile-phone data
show where people actually go, and **agent-based models** reproduce and forecast those flows.

**Vehicles at regional scale.** For the **Emilia-Romagna** region, data from road sensors feed traffic
simulations based on **optimal-velocity models**. These models capture how speed adapts to the
vehicles ahead, and therefore how jams form and dissolve.

## The common thread

Across both case studies the idea is the same: **data on their own describe, models explain and predict**.
Combining the two gives tools that can help design infrastructure, manage crowds and traffic as they
happen, and move toward more sustainable urban mobility.
